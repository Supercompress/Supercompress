Why v2 is different from v1

v1 was a tiny / lightweight compiler: fast on CPU, millisecond-class, good at aggressive drop with answer-keep gates. v2 is a ~400M query-aware cross-encoder — Neural Keep — hosted on Fly (CPU today). It scores every line against this query and keeps original wording.

Critically, it knows when not to compress. Across the full 390-case public suite the aggregate mean cut is only ~3.6%: on dense needle and LongBench slices it often keeps nearly everything. The headline 64.1% is the coding-agent (B5) suite, where dumps are noisy and query-aware keep shines. Mixing those numbers with compiler-era ~47ms CPU claims is wrong — they are different engines.

The problem

Coding agents drown models in tool dumps: logs, diffs, stack traces, RFID heartbeats, the same boot line twelve times. Somewhere in that pile is the one error, ID, or path that answers the question.

Truncation often deletes that line. Summarization rewrites it. Either way you pay for tokens that do not help — or you lose the evidence that does.

What v2 does

Neural Keep is live on POST https://api.supercompress.dev/compress. Hosted requests use the ~400M query-aware engine: it scores each line against this query, keeps original wording, and drops the noise. It does not paraphrase the stack trace. The question itself is never compressed.

That is strongest on coding-agent turns. It also helps on RAG chunks and long support threads when most of the text is not about the ask in front of you.

How we measured

Evidence containment in the compressed text (tiktoken cl100k_base). Pass means required lines are still present in original wording. This is stricter than vibes and weaker than a full downstream completion eval — we say so on purpose.

Limitations

  • Latency: Neural Keep on Fly CPU is seconds-scale on long dumps (~5.8s B5 p50), not the old compiler’s millisecond path.
  • Downstream eval: we have not yet published LLM task-success after compression.
  • Workload fit: strongest on coding-agent / tool-dump noise; conservative when every line could be the needle.
